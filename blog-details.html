The quiet power of daily oiling.

Dr. Ananya Sharma 12 March 2026 6 min read

In Ayurveda, the day begins with a simple act of care: a little warm oil, applied with attention. It is such a small thing that it is easy to overlook — and yet, practiced consistently, it changes how the body feels and how the mind meets the morning.

Abhyanga, or self-oiling, is one of the oldest daily rituals in Indian wellness traditions. The practice is uncomplicated: warm a small amount of oil, then massage it slowly over the body before bathing. The oil is chosen according to your constitution and the season.

Why the oil matters

Oil is not merely a lubricant in Ayurvedic thought. It is considered a carrier of nourishment — a way of delivering the qualities of herbs deep into the tissues. Sesame oil, warmed and infused with ashwagandha, is grounding and restorative. Coconut oil with brahmi is cooling and calming. The right oil for you depends on your dosha and the time of year.

The body remembers care. A few minutes of unhurried touch each morning tells it that it is safe to soften.

A simple morning practice

Begin with a tablespoon of oil warmed gently between your palms. Start at the head, moving slowly down the body with long strokes over the limbs and circular motions at the joints. Pay particular attention to the soles of the feet, the scalp and the abdomen. Five to ten minutes is enough — the quality of attention matters far more than the duration.

If a full-body ritual feels like too much, begin with the feet and hands alone. A practice that is small enough to repeat will always be more powerful than one that is ambitious and abandoned.

What you may notice

Regular self-oiling is traditionally said to support circulation, soften the skin, ease muscular stiffness and calm the nervous system. Many people find that it makes the transition from sleep into activity gentler — a quiet moment of boundary between the private world of rest and the demands of the day.

It is also a useful practice in dry weather, after travel, and during periods of mental overactivity. In each case the principle is the same: warmth, oil and unhurried attention return the body to a sense of ease.

Key takeaways

  • Warm oil before bathing is a simple daily ritual
  • Choose the oil for your dosha and season
  • Five unhurried minutes are enough to begin
  • Consistency matters more than duration
